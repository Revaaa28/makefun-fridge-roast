# Fridge Roast — candidates

Competition: Fridge Roast: One Line, Biggest Laugh (`u-gentle-badger-68-fridge-roast-one-line-biggest-laugh-a7c354bb`), $5 pool.
Judge brief, in order: (1) roast of a smart fridge itself / app / firmware / ice maker;
(2) punchline lands on first read; (3) specific to smart fridges, not a generic gadget gag;
(4) tight wording inside 200 chars; (5) fresh angle — NOT the obvious "orders groceries"
or "watches you" cracks.

## Candidates

1. "My smart fridge has a password. I forgot the password. I am now locked out of my own leftovers." (95 chars)
   Rationale: three-beat escalation; the fridge's absurd security locks its owner out of his own food.
2. "My smart fridge updated its firmware overnight and now the ice maker only works if I agree to the new terms of service." (139 chars)
   Rationale: firmware update + ToS gate on ice — the appliance holds a basic function hostage to legalese.
3. "The ice maker has been 'making ice' since 2019. It's not broken — it's just on strike." (88 chars)
   Rationale: the eternal ice-maker wait, turned with a labour joke; the scare quotes set up the turn.
4. "My fridge needs Wi-Fi to dispense water, so during an outage I own a very expensive, very smug cupboard." (110 chars)
   Rationale: a fridge that stops being a fridge without internet; "smug cupboard" is the laugh.
5. "My smart fridge locked the water dispenser behind a subscription. I now pay monthly to drink from my own kitchen." (120 chars)
   Rationale: subscription absurdity aimed at the dispenser; the turn is paying rent on your own tap water.
6. "My fridge's touchscreen has more apps than my phone and still can't tell me whether the milk is bad." (103 chars)
   Rationale: a $2,000 tablet glued to a fridge that fails the one job a fridge has.
7. "My smart fridge rebooted during a firmware update and forgot what cold is." (76 chars)
   Rationale: shortest line here; the turn is a fridge losing its defining skill to its own update.
8. "The app says my milk expired yesterday. The milk disagrees. I trust the milk." (79 chars)
   Rationale: the fridge's app vs the milk, and the owner sides with the milk — the appliance loses authority.
9. "My fridge's screen shows ads now — I paid $2,000 to be advertised to by my own leftovers." (90 chars)
   Rationale: the door screen as an ad billboard; the buyer is the product in his own kitchen.
10. "I bought a fridge with a camera inside so I'd never lose leftovers. It can see them. It just won't tell me where they are." (124 chars)
    Rationale: the camera knows and withholds — a fridge that watches but won't help.
11. "My smart fridge beeps if I leave the door open ten seconds, like it's the one paying the electric bill." (106 chars)
    Rationale: the nagging door alarm recast as a landlord; relatable, fridge-specific hardware.
12. "My fridge asked me to rate its ice. One star. It has now asked me three more times." (84 chars)
    Rationale: the ice maker begging for a review like a pushy app — the appliance as needy software.

candidate above already beats the field's modal entry on freshness. The ranking below is decided

## Winner

**#1: "My smart fridge has a password. I forgot the password. I am now locked out of my own leftovers."**

It wins the manual ranking: on-subject roast of the fridge itself (criterion 1), the strongest
first-read turn (criterion 2), fridge-specific absurdity (criterion 3), 95 characters with no
wasted word (criterion 4), and a password angle the obvious field never reaches (criterion 5).
Pairwise it beats the ordering-groceries and watching-you shapes on criterion 5 alone, and it
beats our own #2 on tightness and on the human stakes of its punchline.
